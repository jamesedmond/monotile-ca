Showers and ash

seeds that do not settle into a few gliders

Most census seeds die, or launch a few gliders that fly apart. After 150 generations the census set aside every seed that had too many live cells or too many separate pieces to follow one at a time. There were 562 of them among the 3,538 seeds under hat C's rule and 70 among the 2,539 under the spectre rule. Hat A's and hat B's rules had none.

This page follows some of those seeds further. A shower is not one object, so nothing here is certified as a whole. Each viewer runs a seed on the tiles around it, and in the first three every tile that a piece has passed over stays tinted.

A burst

The simplest shower is a burst: the seed breaks into gliders, they fly apart, and nothing else happens. Of the 70 spectre showers, 34 are bursts. Each has between 7 and 21 pieces, the same number at generation 600 as at generation 300, and nothing left near the seed.

Pieces that make more pieces

In the other spectre showers the number of pieces goes on rising after the seed has gone, and the new pieces appear far from it. Of the 70 spectre showers, 35 gained more than two pieces between generations 300 and 600. The largest had 289 pieces at generation 600.

In the run below, the piece travelling left is not one of the certified objects. At about generation 130 it forks: it sheds several gliders, of both the rule's kinds, and carries on along its lane. Followed alone for 2,630 generations it forked eleven times, 252 generations apart except for one gap of 220, and was still travelling at the end. The same kind of object appears in 29 of the 70 spectre showers.

A shower that does not stop

Hat C's showers are different. The place where the seed was stays active and goes on launching gliders. Every ninth one of the 562, which is 63 showers, was followed for 600 generations. In 58 of them there were still live cells within 40 steps of the seed at generation 600, and only 4 were bursts that had finished.

The seed below is two tiles. Followed for 1,500 generations on a larger patch, it has 3,402 live cells in 408 pieces at the end and is still launching, at about one new piece every three generations. Whether it ever stops is not known.

Ash

Not everything at the site travels. Some pieces are small oscillators that stay where they are. The census certified 23 of them under hat C's rule, each a closed set of two to ten situations, so on their own they last forever. At the site of a shower gliders run into them.

Open questions

  • Whether any of hat C's long showers ever stops. The longest run here is 1,500 generations.
  • How a site keeps going. The site has been watched and its pieces counted, but the collisions have not been analysed.
  • Whether the spectre rule's forking traveller goes on forever. It has been followed through eleven forks and has no certificate.
  • Whether hat A's and hat B's rules can shower from larger seeds. None of their 7,076 census seeds did.
