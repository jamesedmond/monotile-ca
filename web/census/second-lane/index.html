The second spectre lane family

two gliders from one seed, 9° apart

The discovery paper's spectre glider travels in one of six directions, 48.014° plus a multiple of 60°. The census found a second glider under the same rule that travels in one of six other directions, 57.170° plus a multiple of 60°. Each direction of the second family is 9.156° from a direction of the first, always on the same side. The census names the two gliders by the number of situations in their certificates: the first is the 2,542 and the second is the 1,454.

Of the 2,539 seeds the census launched under the spectre rule, 401 launch gliders of both families. Sixty of those send a glider of each family along neighbouring lanes, and two of the sixty leave nothing else behind. This page follows one of the two, a random seed of six cells.

One seed, two gliders

The viewer runs the seed for 180 generations. In this record's frame the 2,542 leaves on the 288° lane and the 1,454 on the 297° lane.

The ball around the seed

On a tiling, distance can be counted in steps, where one step goes from a tile to a tile that shares an edge with it. The tiles within n steps of a starting tile are the ball of radius n around it, and the tiles exactly n steps away are its ring n.

Both gliders advance one ring every two generations. They part 35 generations after the seed, and from generation 100 to generation 500 the farthest tile of the 2,542 is never behind the farthest tile of the 1,454 and never more than three rings ahead of it. At any generation the two gliders are therefore on the outline of nearly the same ball.

A large ball on the spectre tiling is not round. Its outline has six major corners and six minor corners, with each minor corner about 9° past a major one. The 2,542 travels towards a major corner and the 1,454 towards the minor corner next to it.

A major corner is farther from the seed than a minor corner, so in the same number of generations the 2,542 covers more ground. In this run it moves 1.584 units per generation and the 1,454 moves 1.549. A unit is the length of a spectre's edge.

What is proved and what is measured

Each glider's certificate proves that it travels forever and that its long-run heading lies inside the window in the table. The two windows do not overlap, so the two families are proved to be different directions.

The match between the lanes and the corners of the ball is measured. On a ball of radius 384 the corners sit within 0.05° of the lanes. There is no proof that a glider's lane must be a corner of the ball.

The hat tiling's ball has one kind of corner, and every hat glider in the census travels towards it.
