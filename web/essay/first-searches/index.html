First searches

the mortal era — five upgrades of expressiveness, and a ceiling

Enumerating rules was never the hard part; the census ran in seconds. The work is deciding what each run was — exact periods by cycle detection, the explosion filter, and above all a way to tell a glider from its imitations. This section is the story of that machinery being forged, by a sequence of objects that each nearly qualified.

A million runs before lunch

Soups are the wrong instrument for finding travellers; small seeds are better. Any tile's address can serve as a patch root, so the search re-roots at a representative cell of every tile class — 17 on the hat, 10 on the spectre — and launches every B0-free rule from ~10 small seeds each: the single cell, each cell-plus-neighbour pair, the 1-ball, the ring. That is 1.2 million runs on the hat (21 seconds), 3.3 million on the spectre (49 seconds). Six runs were flagged as glider candidates: population never above 64, exiting the patch with all activity still outbound.

Anatomy of the traveller

The traveller is a mechanism, and an isolated one. Of all 433 rules within three bit-flips of B25/S25, only the two originals travel 20 rings at all; the nearest variants manage 15, 14, 13, 12 — and several die into the same period-2 blinker, at the same generation. Launch site matters as much as rule: from six of the 17 anchor classes it dies within 28 generations; from three it travels 11–23 rings first. Every run, everywhere, ends the same way — the blinker calves off, anchors the reaction, and the traveller evaporates around it.

The grower that fooled the referee

The strongest k-state object very nearly slipped through. Visually it is a swarm of miniature gliders: each begins as a single bright cell that splits to two, holds together for a few steps — sometimes aided by a third head cell — then forks apart, the pair sharing a wake a while longer. Across the whole swarm only 6–44 cells are firing at any moment; everything else is ember wake.

Checked at radii 120 through 168 its peak population sat flat at 140 — bounded, apparently. Checked at 128, 256, 384 it read 140, 186, 280: growth, too slow for closely-spaced radii to see. The fix is multiplicative spacing — verify at r, 2r, 4r — and it became part of the standard battery. The grower itself was run to radius 1024 (3.6 million tiles) and never fizzled: where the single-front traveller died at ring 23, the forker survives because a front lost in hostile terrain is replaced by its siblings. What disqualifies it as a glider — growth — is precisely what keeps it alive.

The ceiling

The travel record, by rule expressiveness: 23 rings uniform, 28 on the spectre, 37 with the two-coloring, 58 and 65 per-class, ~82 with k states — monotone, flattening, and never an escape. The era's verdict, exactly scoped: no glider on either tiling by any 2- to 5-state semi-totalistic rule, uniform or fully class-stratified, within reach of these searches. Three ways to read it: the rule family is too weak; the searches were; or — the pessimistic one — these tilings simply do not support gliders, and no rule family will do. Nothing in the data could rule the third out. We pressed on anyway: the next section pushes the search as hard as it will go, and the one after tests the rule family against the one published glider native to an aperiodic tiling.
