The hunt

the identical pipeline, pointed home

Everything validated on the Penrose tilings came home. The monotile search moved into the new space — vertex adjacency (degrees 6–7 here, smaller than P3's 7–11) and 4-state table rules — while the pipeline itself was carried over untouched: the same GA, the same random initialisation and seed bank, the same travel × flatness objective, cap and cross-radius battery, at the calibrated scale of 256 × 800. Neither substrate has any analogue of P3's straight ribbons. Six independent runs. Four produced verified gliders — three on the hat, one on the spectre:

Run names are the search's bookkeeping: hat-tableevolve-r48-s21 is the table-rule GA on a radius-48 hat patch with random seed 21, and s21, s22, … are those seeds. Distances stay in edge-rings throughout, even on these vertex-adjacency patches.

Each is ReachedBoundary at radius 48, 96, 192, 384 and 768 — a 16× span, 765 rings of aperiodic terrain survived (the verifier stops three rings short of the edge, the vertex fan bound) — with max population exactly flat at every radius. These are the first travelling objects beyond mortal travellers and growers ever observed on the aperiodic monotilings and, to our knowledge, the first gliders on any substrate without straight rails. And where P3 held out for ≈115,000 evaluations, the hat gave up s21 at GA generation 5 — about 1,300 evaluations. Once the rule family and the neighbourhood were right, gliders were not rare at all. Months of "no" had been aimed at the wrong space.

Multi-glider births are typical. s21 opens with two parallel gliders travelling together; at generation 42 one clips the other's wake and dies, and the survivor settles onto its lane — a fixed bearing it then holds. The spectre's s33 launches a clean pair separating at ~120°:

A wanderer as an excited bound state that sheds gliders — direct visual evidence the two classes are kin, and the origin of s23's 62-generation startup cost:

The arrival laws are predictive out of sample: fitted on radii up to 384, they forecast the radius-768 arrivals to 0.25% — hat C's law, generation = 62 + 2r, predicts 1598 against a measured 1602, and the clean gliders arrive at 1533/1533/1534 against a predicted ≈1536. Every verified glider, on both families — and Goucher's — runs the same ~2 generations per ring: the birth-ahead, die-behind relay seems to set a universal clock. (The phoenix is the exception that locates the limit: with no survival rows at all it is reborn every generation, and runs at one.)

The two that were not gliders

The remaining spectre runs are the anti-gaming machinery paying rent. s31 was a grower — max population 50 → 207 → 1,165 across the verification radii, invisible in-patch, exposed by the multiplicative spread. s32, the looper, is a near-miss for the ages:

Period 3,300 — the lcm of 60 and 550 — is the longest exact recurrence the project has recorded, and it is the beat of two independent captures, not one orbit: cycle detection on the full state certifies recurrence, but says nothing about how many objects the state contains. The lesson cuts both ways: the machinery works, and immortality is empirical — this family can travel ~190 rings and then trap, so arbitrarily-late failure stays a live possibility for every glider in the table above.

What made the difference

All three levers the validation control identified, jointly: a rule family whose non-live states are visible to neighbours and whose conditions conjoin (the winning rules use both — the missing critical point of the Generations era was a property of the rule family, not the substrates); vertex adjacency; and calibrated search power. Each is individually necessary — a later section shows the ablation, along with a brand-new way the search tried to cheat. And one thing was not necessary: rails. The monotiles have no straight ribbons, yet every glider appears to hold the universal clock and a fixed bearing — s21 runs dead south-west (225.523°; headings throughout are measured counter-clockwise from east), straight at a corner of the hexagonal patch. Appears is doing honest work: 768 rings is the longest flight yet verified, and both the clock and the immortality want far longer rides — which is the business of Tiling on the fly. The bearings — confirming they really do quantize, and what in the geometry might set them — belong to The survey.
