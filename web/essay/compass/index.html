The survey

the million-ring measurements: the clock, the lanes, and an exact compass law

The instrument keeps three distances distinct. Path length — hop-to-hop graph distance summed — is an upper bound on how far from home. Euclidean displacement is the primary global metric: consecutive windows share tiles by address, the shared tiles give the exact rigid transform between frames, and the transforms compose in double precision — residual ~10⁻¹³, every rotation landing on the tiling's 30° orientation set to 0.0000°. Effective rings convert displacement back to the origin-anchored radius through κ, the substrate's rings-per-unit constant, fitted offset-free. Headings are estimated twice — the settled heading (circular mean over the flight's late half) and the net heading (bearing of the total displacement) — and the two agree to under 0.01° on every lane. Three things in that table deserve a long look. The clock: every lane on both substrates runs 1.99–2.00 generations per effective ring, the universal relay pace, now held across a million rings. The coincidence: hat A and hat B are unrelated genomes — different rules, found by different runs — and their settled headings agree to three decimal places. And the arithmetic: hat C's lane sits 120.001° from A's, and the spectre's twin lanes sit 240.000° apart. Exact multiples of sixty, from unrelated rules whose only common ingredient is the board — the spacing must be the substrate's, not the rules'.

The compass law

Fit one number per substrate — a fan of six spokes spaced exactly 60° apart, with a free offset — and every measured lane lands on a spoke with residual ~0.000°, inside each lane's standard error: hat offset 45.523°, spectre offset 48.014°. The caveat, stated straight: the observed lanes are themselves 60°-multiples apart, so a single-offset fit is self-consistent rather than overdetermined. The parameter-free content is the exact 120.001° and 240.000° separations, and the fact that one stable offset per substrate drives every residual to zero — across independent genomes, independent objects, and from 10⁵ to 10⁶ rings. The law inherits its exactness from the geometry: rotations that land on the orientation set to 0.0000° leave nothing for heading error to hide in.

Filling the fan

Six spokes, five lanes — and a fair objection: every discovered object was seeded at the same two tiles, so perhaps the launch site, not the rule, picked the directions. Re-seeding answers both halves. Sweeping each rule from an interior anchor of every tile class, across every shared edge, in both orders: 89% of 188 arbitrary two-tile seedings launch a flat-population traveller for hat A, 88% for hat B, 71% for the spectre — and across ~490 seedings of those three rules, not one death and not one trap. (Hat C's 24% fits its wanderer-intermediate mechanism; the screening label is a lower bound.) Ignition is a property of the rules, not of the discovery site — and different sites launch different directions. The lanes are the metric's fast axes

Where do the spokes point? Every trail figure ends at a corner of the patch's pseudo-hexagonal outline, which suggested a hypothesis: the lane directions are the corner directions of the graph-metric ball — the directions in which one ring of graph distance buys the most Euclidean ground. Measuring that is delicate — the ball's corners turn out to be sharp, asymmetric V-cusps, and smoothing-based estimators mislocate them (on the spectre, by 2.6°) — but fitting the cusps as intersecting flank lines puts all twelve cusps on the lane directions: hat −0.13° ± 0.03°, spectre −0.11° ± 0.01°, residuals at the known systematic floor of a radius-384 patch (the flights measured the lanes on baselines 3,000× longer). And the rates agree: κ at the cusp matches the flight κ to a few parts per thousand. The gliders travel the substrate's fast axes at the substrate's rate. The compass phase is a metric constant of the tiling.

Two footnotes from that measurement. First, do not confuse the metric ball with the growth fronts of The game on the board: the ball is far from round — κ varies ~15% with direction on the hat, ~12% on the spectre — while chaotic growth fronts are dynamically smoothed to within ~1% of circular. Two different objects; the contrast is why the round fronts were striking in the first place. Second, the spectre's cusps are chiral — steep on one flank, shallow on the other, where the hat's are symmetric to ~2% — the tiling's handedness, visible in the shape of its metric ball.

What the survey does not explain is the offsets themselves. 45.523° and 48.014° are measured constants, not derived ones; the question they pose — the cusp directions of the BFS-ball limit shape, a pure substrate-geometry object plausibly computable from the substitution — appears to be new. The numbers are on the table for whoever computes them.
