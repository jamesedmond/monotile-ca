Towards a proof

situations, saturation, and the closure that remains

A million rings is a measurement, not a theorem. The question this section poses is whether verified to a million rings can be upgraded to immortal on every legal tiling — and two facts make that conceivable rather than hopeless. A substitution tiling has finite local complexity: up to isomorphism, only finitely many neighbourhoods of any fixed size occur (The board's recurrence viewer was this fact in action). And one step of a cellular automaton is strictly local. Together they reduce a proof to bookkeeping — enumerate what the glider can encounter, and show the set closes.

The bookkeeping unit is the situation: a snapshot of the glider as the rule sees it — the non-quiescent cells together with the substrate collar within a fixed graph radius of them, tile classes, adjacencies and vertex degrees included, canonically encoded so that two snapshots are equal exactly when their collared neighbourhoods are isomorphic, wherever on the tiling they occur:

The step map reads only the collar — that is what "the rule is local" means — but the next situation's collar reaches one ring beyond the current one, into terrain the current situation does not determine. Each legal way that fresh ring can be tiled is a collar extension, and a certificate must show the glider steps safely, with positive displacement, under every one of them. That is the whole proof obligation: a finite set of situations, closed under the step map, for every legal extension. The figures form a ladder in collar radius: with no collar the surroundings vary wildly; two rings in, the observed geometry is already forced and only the hidden class labels differ; at the certificate's radius 4, the step map itself is determined.

The vocabulary is finite, and it saturates hard

Finiteness of the situation set was guaranteed by finite local complexity; the scientific content is its size and shape. Counting distinct situations along hat A's flight at the step-sufficient collar radius: 167. The last new one appears at generation 434, and novelty arrives in bursts — hierarchical terrain, not a steady drip — separated by long dead stretches:

The control is the doubled window: on a patch twice the radius, 1,074 consecutive further generations of entirely fresh terrain produce nothing new. Branching — situations with more than one observed continuation — is confined to four situations with exactly two continuations each, and their recurrence gaps carry additive structure (178, 288, and 466 = 178 + 288): the signature of a substitutive sequence, where the terrain is built by inflation and return distances at successive levels add, the way Fibonacci lengths do. The spectre glider gives the same picture with a vocabulary of 306. One subtlety: vocabularies are per-lane. A wanderer of hat A's own rule, launched from a different site, was captured onto the 345° lane and flown 30,000 rings: by every macroscopic invariant it is then a hat glider, yet it shares not one situation with hat A's atlas — so a certificate covers one rule–lane pair, and six certificates cover a substrate.

The encoding itself is checked, not trusted. The same-radius successor map is not forced to be deterministic even by a perfect encoding (the next collar sees terrain the key does not), so the soundness control is the forced map — from a larger collar to a smaller successor — and all six such maps (collar 4 to 2; 5 to 2 and 3; 6 to 2, 3 and 4) are deterministic on every sampled generation. What remains as same-radius "nondeterminism" is then pure collar-extension branching: exactly the thing the certificate must close over.

What remains

The certificate is therefore no longer a conjecture about whether a finite description exists — it measurably does — but a bounded enumeration task: generate the legal collar continuations of the observed situations from the substitution hierarchy, and verify closure of the graph, attending to the four branch points. Two scope notes on that set. The 167 is hat A's — one rule–lane pair of the six a substrate carries. And the observed list is a size estimate, not a precondition: the enumeration is self-completing — any situation it uncovers simply joins the set under test — and could in principle be seeded from a single mid-flight situation, closure discovering everything reachable from it under every legal extension, with the verified flight supplying the bridge from launch to seed.

It is worth being equally precise about what the finished theorem would say. It would prove that a glider, once settled — once it has entered any situation of the closed set — keeps its lane forever: the speed and the bearing become theorems. It would not prove that every travelling object of this rule must ride these lanes; the other five spokes of the fan need their own certificates, and objects outside the atlas entirely — never observed, but never excluded — are beyond its scope. Immortality of what we have; silence about what we have not.

Two structural gifts make the task tractable, and one hazard keeps it honest. Over-approximation is safe in exactly one direction: proving survival on a superset of the legal extensions proves survival on the tiling, so the substitution hierarchy is needed only where lazy enumeration fails — but an illegal extension can also manufacture phantom situations that occur on no real tiling, and a phantom dying is a false alarm, not a disproof. And the compass reduces the terrain ahead to a corridor whose collar sequence is itself substitutive, so the certificate should factor into the glider's small phase machine crossed with a finite corridor automaton — Goucher's ribbon argument reappearing as the special case where the corridor alphabet has one letter: a ribbon is the same collar forever, which is all his argument needs. Finally, one suspected symmetry could shrink the job six-fold: the dynamics are blind to the tile classes the encoding records, so under a class-blind encoding the six lanes' machines — 60° rotations of one another, with identical clocks and speeds — may turn out to coincide, collapsing a substrate's six certificates into one. Suspected, motivated, designated the first step; not yet shown.

That is where this project stands: a glider that measurably inhabits 167 situations, a step map that is deterministic wherever it is forced to be, four branch points with substitutive structure, and an enumeration nobody has yet run. The last section hands you the instruments.
