Genetic search

the objective is the hard part

Enumeration ends at one rule table. With a table per tile class — seventeen on the hat — the space is ~2150, and search has to become evolution: a genome is the list of tables, crossover swaps whole classes between parents, mutation flips single birth or survival bits, tournaments pick winners, and every run is deterministic given its random seed. What "winner" means is the rest of this section.

You get what you reward

Reward reaching the boundary, and the GA "wins" with the known 58-ring mortal — on any patch shorter than its lifespan, a dying traveller escapes and scores. Reward distance under a population cap, and it breeds a filament slow enough to stay under the cap for the whole crossing, then balloon: max population 93 at radius 64, 1,665 at 128, 3,407 at 192. Each parasite bred a countermeasure — distance-only fitness, an explosion filter that aborts any run passing a population ceiling, and above all cross-radius verification: re-run every champion on bigger boards. A glider keeps escaping; a mortal plateaus; a grower's population climbs with the board. No result in this project stands without that battery, and this section is where it was earned.

The balance point

The mortal era left two kinds of object: travellers, which lose population and die, and growers, which gain it and live. A glider is net-zero — so the endgame searched the death↔growth boundary directly, seeding the GA with the grower itself and rewarding flat-population travel. Two fitness refinements, each closing the previous loophole, converged on the same kind of champion:

Linear every time: double the board, double the population — a glider would hold constant. Each refinement thinned the grower and none reached zero, because a fitness evaluated at radius R only sees growth that shows up within R, and there is always a grower just under that threshold. The boundary was being approached as a limit with no member at it — a missing critical point.

The floor test

One question remained: does the thinning continue forever, or is there a floor? Doubling the span — radius 400, same cap, same flatness pressure — settled it. Over the longer haul the search could not sustain a grower at all. Its champion is something the era had not produced before:

Flat, and mortal. That splits the boundary into two regimes: structures that live forever grow (at ~0.28 cells per ring or faster, within reach of these searches), and structures that hold a bounded population die (within ~100 rings). A glider — immortal and flat — is exactly the corner neither regime touches. This is the strongest statement of the era: across the exhaustive 2-state census, the bit-flip sweeps, two-coloring (chirality) and full per-class stratification, two to five states, and a targeted balance search, nothing sits on that corner.

It is not a proof. It is one rule family and a finite amount of search, and "within reach of these searches" is doing real work in every sentence above. How much search would be enough to trust a negative like this one? That question has an answer, and the next section buys it with a control experiment on a tiling where the ground truth is known.
